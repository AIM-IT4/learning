Day 12/30 · Making Quant Easy: "CVA in 16 lines" (Desk2Quant code-along lesson reel, 103.2 s, 1080 x 1920, 24 fps): credits

Voice: assets/vo.wav (= voice/out/vo_tight.wav, 101.10 s, loudnormed), placed at t = 0.12 s: ElevenLabs text-to-speech,
  the stock voice "Eric" (not a clone of a real person), tightened as supplied (every pause over 0.36 s cut to 0.30 s;
  same words and speed); not regenerated or edited here. Word timings: voice/out/words.json. The natural take is kept as
  voice/out/vo_v1_natural.wav (words_v1_natural.json).
Code on screen: work/cva_reel.py (16 lines), shown character for character (inlined by tools/compose.py).
Numbers on screen: work/numbers.json, written by work/numbers.py, which runs work/cva_reel.py (10,000 simulated paths,
  seed 7, monthly grid). Illustrative model, labelled "simulated" / "illustrative" on screen: receive-fixed 5-year swap
  at par, value = -N x (T - t) x (rate move), Brownian rate moves with 1% a year normal vol, flat hazard from the credit
  triangle, flat 3% discounting, no netting, collateral or wrong-way risk (work/facts.json, model_note).
The 2008 statistic: Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, press release of 1 June 2011
  (https://www.bis.org/press/p110601.htm): "During the financial crisis, roughly two-thirds of losses attributed to
  counterparty credit risk were due to CVA losses and only about one-third were due to actual defaults." Shown as
  2/3 and 1/3 with the source line "Source: Basel Committee (BCBS), June 2011" (work/facts.json).
Illustrative drawing: the small rising credit-spread sparkline in the hook (a made-up shape with no numbers), labelled
  "illustrative". The b10 chart draws shapes only (√t, T − t and their product, each as a share of its maximum).
Logo: Desk2Quant's own (assets/logo.png, the same file as in d2q-mr-promo), in the brand pill and as the DM sender.
  No other brand, bank or employer name or logo; no character or presenter. Everything else is drawn in code
  (work/kit.js, work/scenes/).
