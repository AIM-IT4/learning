You know how to delta-hedge. Then the interviewer asks: your hedge is perfect, so why does your P&L still move every day? 🎯

Day 10/30 · Making Quant Easy

1️⃣ The setup: say you own a call. The stock is at 100, the strike is 100, volatility is 20%, and it expires in one year. Delta is 0.54, so you hedge by shorting 0.54 shares.
2️⃣ Stock +2%: the call gains 1.12, the hedge loses 1.08. Stock −2%: the call loses 1.04, the hedge gains 1.08. Net +0.04 either way. That's gamma: the call's price is a curve, so delta grows as the stock rises (0.58 at 102) and shrinks as it falls (0.50 at 98).
3️⃣ The cost is theta. One day later, at the same stock, the call is worth 7.95 instead of 7.97. On 1,000 calls that's about ₹15.75 a day.
4️⃣ The breakeven: gamma pays more than the rent when the stock moves more than σ/√252 = 1.26% in a day. That's the rule of 16: 20% ÷ 16 ≈ 1.25%.
5️⃣ Simulate it, hedging every day for a year. Averaged over 2,000 simulated years, 1,000 calls end near ₹0 if the stock really moves 20% a year, about +₹3,964 at 30%, and about −₹3,958 at 10%.

🎤 Quant interview line: "A delta-hedged call earns gamma on big moves and pays theta every day. It breaks even when the stock moves σ/√252 a day, 1.26% here. So its P&L is realised volatility against the implied volatility you paid."
